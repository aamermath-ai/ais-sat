ACT guide

The enhanced ACT (from 2025): shorter, with an optional Science section and a composite made from English, Math and Reading.

Test format

SectionQuestionsTimeWhat it tests
English5035 minProduction of Writing (about 40%), Knowledge of Language (about 20%), Conventions of Standard English (about 40%), all in passages with underlined portions
Math4550 minNumber and quantity, algebra, functions, geometry, statistics and probability, plus essential skills and modeling. 4 answer choices; calculator allowed (Desmos is built into the online test)
Reading3640 minKey ideas and details, craft and structure, integration of knowledge and ideas, across 4 passages (literary narrative, social science, humanities, natural science)
Science (optional)4040 minInterpreting data, scientific investigation, and evaluating models and conflicting viewpoints
AIST Math 1 (separate test)5060 minACT International Subject Test: algebra and geometry (equations, functions, data, areas and volumes, proofs and logic). Calculator allowed.
Writing (optional)1 essay40 minAn argumentative essay, scored separately

Scores: each section 1–36. Composite = average of English, Math and Reading. Taking Science also gives a Science score and a STEM score (average of Math and Science). There is no penalty for wrong answers, so answer every question.

Colleges in the US accept either test equally. Try a full practice test of each, then focus on the one that suits you.

English strategies

  • Read the whole sentence, not just the underlined part.
  • Shorter is often better: cut repeated words.
  • "NO CHANGE" is right only when nothing is wrong. Don't fear it, but check it.
  • For "Should the writer add…" questions, ask whether the idea fits the paragraph's purpose.

Math strategies

  • Memorise key formulas: the ACT gives none.
  • Questions get harder as you go, so bank time on the early ones.
  • Use Desmos for graphs, systems and checking answers.
  • Learn the extra topics: matrices, logs, complex numbers, unit-circle trig.

Reading strategies

  • Aim for about 10 minutes per passage.
  • Read for the main idea and how each paragraph works.
  • Every right answer is supported by the text; eliminate choices that go beyond it.
  • For paired passages, answer each passage's questions before the comparison questions.

Science strategies

  • It's mostly reading graphs and tables; little outside knowledge is needed.
  • Go to the figure the question names, then read the axes and units.
  • Look for trends: increase, decrease, peak.
  • In conflicting viewpoints, summarise each scientist in one line.
